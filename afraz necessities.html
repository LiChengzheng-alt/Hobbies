Football Necessities

The basic gear you need to train and play, plus what I personally use.

My Go-To Gear

My favourite boots to play in are the adidas Predators. I like how they grip the ball when passing and shooting, and they feel solid for a full 90 minutes without hurting my feet. Here's a review video I found useful when I was deciding on a pair:

Watch: adidas Predator 26 Elite Boot Review (YouTube)

I also always wear a headband when I play - mine's the Nike Dri-FIT Headband. It's a small thing but it stops sweat from running down into my eyes, especially in longer matches or hot weather - makes it a lot easier to actually see the ball late in the game.

Gear Comparison Table

A quick breakdown of the main gear categories you'll need and what each one is actually for.

Gear What It's For Examples Price Range
Boots Grip on the pitch, ball control, and striking power. Different studs suit different surfaces (firm ground, artificial turf, indoor). adidas Predator, Nike Mercurial, Puma Future $50 - $300
Headgear Keeps sweat and hair out of your eyes so you can actually see the ball. Some players also use it for light impact protection. Nike Dri-FIT Headband, generic sports headband, moisture-wicking cap $5 - $20
Socks Hold your shin guards in place and reduce blisters. Long football socks are worn over the shin guards, not under. Football socks, compression socks $5 - $25
Shin Guards Basic protection for your shins against kicks and tackles. Required in most competitive matches. Slip-in guards, ankle guards $10 - $40
Gloves Mainly for goalkeepers - better grip on the ball and some padding when catching or diving. Outfield players sometimes wear thin gloves in cold weather. Goalkeeper gloves, thermal gloves $15 - $80
